REVIEW · DEFINITION

What are carbohydrates?

Carbohydrates are aldehyde or ketone derivatives of polyhydric alcohols — or any substance derived from them.

REVIEW · INTRODUCTION

Importance

⚡ Most abundant
The most abundant class of organic compounds on the surface of the earth.
✔ Elemental makeup
Contain the elements carbon, hydrogen and oxygen (C, H, O).
💧 Chemical nature
Poly-hydroxy aldehydes or poly-hydroxy ketones — or their derivatives.
🌿 Made by photosynthesis
Produced by photosynthesis: H₂O + CO₂.
🔥 Universal fuel
All animals rely on carbohydrate as a source of energy.

REVIEW · MONOSACCHARIDES

How monosaccharides are classified

By active carbonyl group

Aldoses contain an aldehyde group (–CHO).

Ketoses contain a ketone group (C=O).

The active carbonyl carbon is the anomeric carbon — C1 in aldoses, C2 in ketoses.

By number of carbon atoms

Trioses3C
Tetroses4C
Pentoses5C
Hexoses6C
Heptoses7C
REVIEW · MONOSACCHARIDES

Trioses & Tetroses

The suffix “-ulose” marks a keto sugar (e.g. erythrulose).
Trioses are the smallest possible monosaccharides.

Hexoses · C₆H₁₂O₆

Hexoses are the most common monosaccharides in nature.
Dashed box: mannose differs from glucose at C2, galactose at C4.

Heptoses · C₇H₁₄O₇

Active group = anomeric carbon: C1 in aldoses, C2 in ketoses.

01 · RING STRUCTURE

Ring (cyclic) structure

The open-chain formula can't explain every reaction of a sugar — the –CHO group must be masked.
In solution, the –OH of the hydrated aldehyde reacts with the –OH of C4 or C5 and closes into a ring (hemiacetal).
02 · HAWORTH

Haworth formula

A flat ring drawing of the sugar.
–OH on the right in the old ring → drawn down.
–OH on the left → drawn up.
Reversed at the CH₂OH group.
03 · ASYMMETRIC CARBON

Asymmetric carbon atom

A carbon atom attached to 4 different groups or atoms.
Any substance containing one shows 2 properties: optical activity and optical isomerism.
04 · OPTICAL ACTIVITY

Optical activity

The ability of a substance to rotate plane-polarized light to the right or to the left.
Right = dextrorotatory (d or +)
Left = levorotatory (l or −)
05 · OPTICAL ISOMERISM

Optical isomerism

The ability of a substance to exist in more than one form (isomers).
Simplest example: glyceraldehyde — D and L forms that are mirror images (enantiomers).

06-A · SUGAR DERIVATIVES

Sugar acids

Produced by oxidation of the carbonyl carbon, the last hydroxyl carbon, or both.
06-B · SUGAR DERIVATIVES

Sugar alcohols

Sugars reduced at the carbonyl carbon to the corresponding alcohol.
06-C · SUGAR DERIVATIVES

Deoxysugars

One hydroxyl group is replaced by a hydrogen atom — one oxygen is missing.
06-D · SUGAR DERIVATIVES

Amino sugars

The –OH on carbon 2 is replaced by an amino (or acetylamino) group.

07 · GLYCOSIDIC BOND

Glycosidic bond

Bond between a carbohydrate and another compound.
Formed at the anomeric carbon (C1 in aldoses, C2 in ketoses).
Partner: a monosaccharide (maltose, lactose, sucrose) or an aglycone.
